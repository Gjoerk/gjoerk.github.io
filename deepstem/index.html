Install

Takes about a minute. Both installers include everything DeepStem needs.

  1. Download DeepStem-Windows-Setup.exe with the button above and open it.
  2. If your browser or Windows warns about the file: in Edge choose … → Keep → Show more → Keep anyway; if “Windows protected your PC” appears, click More info → Run anyway. The beta isn’t code-signed yet, so Windows doesn’t know the publisher. Prefer no installer? Use the portable ZIP and copy DeepStem.vst3 into C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3.
  3. Click through the installer. The plug-in goes to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3, the standard folder every DAW scans. The DeepStem app appears in the Start menu.
  4. Rescan plug-ins in your DAW (in FL Studio: Options → Manage plugins → Find plugins), then load DeepStem. It’s listed as an instrument.
  1. Download DeepStem-macOS.pkg with the button above.
  2. Open it. If macOS says it “can’t be opened because Apple cannot check it”, click Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to DeepStem. This appears because the beta isn’t notarized by Apple yet.
  3. Click through the installer and enter your Mac password when asked. The plug-in goes to /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 and the app to Applications.
  4. Rescan plug-ins in your DAW, then load DeepStem. It’s listed as an instrument. Logic Pro and GarageBand don’t support VST3: use the DeepStem app instead.

How to use it

The same steps in the plug-in and in the standalone app.

  1. Drop a song onto the window, or press Choose file. WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3 and OGG work everywhere; M4A/AAC on macOS.
  2. Pick 2 or 4 stems, a format (WAV or FLAC) and a quality: Standard is fastest, High and Ultra take longer.
  3. Press Separate. A progress bar shows the time left, measured on your computer.
  4. Preview with play, solo stems with S, and click any waveform to jump there.
  5. Drag a stem’s handle into your project, or open the output folder at the bottom right. Stems are saved in Music/DeepStem Stems/<song>.

FAQ

How long does a song take?

It depends on your computer. As a rough guide, a 3½-minute song takes about 1½ minutes on Standard on an Apple M1, and longer on older laptops. High and Ultra are slower. DeepStem shows the real time left while it works.

Why does Windows or my browser warn about the download?

Windows SmartScreen and Defender warn about programs from publishers they don’t know yet. DeepStem’s beta installer isn’t code-signed, so it gets this warning even though it’s clean. A signed version is coming. Until then you can click through the warning as described above, or use the portable ZIP, which contains just the plug-in and needs no installer.

Does it need the internet?

No. Everything runs on your computer and your audio is never uploaded.

Which DAWs does it work in?

Any DAW that loads VST3 plug-ins: FL Studio, Ableton Live, Cubase, Studio One, Reaper, Bitwig and more. For Logic Pro, GarageBand or no DAW at all, use the standalone DeepStem app.

I don’t see DeepStem in my DAW

Rescan your plug-ins and look under instruments, not effects. Check the plug-in is in your VST3 folder: C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3 on Windows, /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 on macOS.
